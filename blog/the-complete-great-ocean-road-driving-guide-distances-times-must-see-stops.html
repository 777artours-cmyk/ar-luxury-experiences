The Great Ocean Road: An Overview

Stretching approximately 243 kilometers (151 miles) along the southeastern coast of Victoria, the Great Ocean Road officially begins in Torquay and concludes near Allansford, just west of Warrnambool. While it's technically possible to drive the entire length in about 3.5 to 4 hours without stopping, we strongly advise against it. To truly appreciate its wonders, allow a minimum of two full days, with three days being ideal.

Essential Stops & Driving Segments

To help you plan, we've broken down the journey into key segments, highlighting the distances, approximate driving times (without stops), and unmissable attractions.

Segment 1: Torquay to Lorne

  • Distance: Approximately 45 km
  • Driving Time: 1 hour
  • Highlights:
    • Torquay: The surfing capital of Australia, home to Bells Beach, a world-renowned surf break. Great for a quick photo or to soak in the surf culture.
    • Bells Beach: Iconic surf spot, even if you don't surf, the views are spectacular.
    • Split Point Lighthouse (Aireys Inlet): A picturesque lighthouse offering incredible ocean views and a popular photo opportunity.

Segment 2: Lorne to Apollo Bay

  • Distance: Approximately 45 km
  • Driving Time: 1 hour
  • Highlights:
    • Teddy's Lookout (Lorne): Just a short drive from Lorne, this lookout provides a magnificent panorama of the Great Ocean Road winding into the distance and the Saint George River.
    • Sheoak Falls: A short, easy walk leads to these beautiful cascading falls, offering a tranquil break from the coastal drive.
    • Kennett River: Famous for its abundant koala population. Keep an eye on the eucalyptus trees along the Grey River Road – you're almost guaranteed to spot one!

Segment 3: Apollo Bay to Port Campbell (The Shipwreck Coast)

This is arguably the most dramatic and iconic section of the Great Ocean Road, where the coastline transforms into rugged cliffs and incredible rock formations. Allow ample time here; actual sightseeing will take several hours.

  • Distance: Approximately 90 km
  • Driving Time: 1.5 - 2 hours (driving only)
  • Highlights:
    • Maits Rest Rainforest Walk: A magical, easy 30-minute loop walk through ancient rainforest, offering a stark contrast to the coastal scenery.
    • Cape Otway Lighthouse: (Detour: ~30km return from Great Ocean Road) Australia's most significant lighthouse, offering stunning views and a glimpse into maritime history.
    • Gibson Steps: Descend 86 steps to the beach below for a unique perspective of the towering cliffs and two of the Twelve Apostles.
    • Twelve Apostles: The undisputed highlight. These magnificent limestone stacks rising from the Southern Ocean are best viewed at sunrise or sunset for truly spectacular colors.
    • Loch Ard Gorge: A dramatic gorge with a fascinating shipwreck history. Explore its various viewpoints, including Razorback and Island Archway.
    • The Grotto: A unique geological formation featuring a sinkhole, archway, and calm pool, offering a serene viewing experience.
    • London Arch (London Bridge): Once a natural double-span bridge, now a single arch following a collapse in 1990. Still incredibly impressive.

Segment 4: Port Campbell to Warrnambool (or Allansford)

  • Distance: Approximately 60 km
  • Driving Time: 1 hour
  • Highlights:
    • Bay of Islands: Similar to the Twelve Apostles but often less crowded, offering equally stunning rock formations.
    • Bay of Martyrs: A beautiful, tranquil bay with a poignant history.
    • Warrnambool: The largest city at the western end of the Great Ocean Road. If visiting between June and September, head to Logan's Beach to spot Southern Right Whales.

Planning Your Great Ocean Road Adventure

  • Duration: While a single long day is possible, 2-3 days allows for a relaxed pace, more stops, and enjoying sunrises/sunsets.
  • Best Time to Visit: Spring (Sept-Nov) and Autumn (March-May) offer milder weather and fewer crowds. Summer (Dec-Feb) is popular but can be very busy.
  • What to Pack: Layers of clothing (weather can change rapidly), comfortable walking shoes, sunscreen, hat, camera, and plenty of water.
  • Driving Tips: Drive on the left. Be aware of wildlife, especially at dawn and dusk. Fuel stations are available in larger towns. Don't rush – the journey is the destination.
