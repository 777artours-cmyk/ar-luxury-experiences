Discover London Arch Great Ocean Road: History, Facts & Ultimate Viewing Tips with AR Tours

Once known as London Bridge, the magnificent London Arch stands as a testament to nature's power on Victoria's iconic Great Ocean Road. Delve into its fascinating past, uncover intriguing facts, and learn how to best experience its breathtaking beauty.

Once a majestic double-arched bridge, its dramatic transformation in 1990 captivated the world, renaming it from "London Bridge" to the solitary "London Arch." A Glimpse into the Past: The History of London Arch

For millennia, the relentless forces of wind and waves sculpted the limestone cliffs, carving out what was once known as London Bridge. This natural wonder was a spectacular two-span arch, resembling its namesake in London, connected to the mainland. Tourists could walk across the outer span to the farthest section, enjoying unparalleled ocean views.

Then, on January 15, 1990, without warning, the section of the arch connected to the mainland dramatically collapsed. Two tourists found themselves stranded on the newly formed "island," requiring a helicopter rescue. Miraculously, no one was seriously injured, but the event forever changed the landscape. The magnificent double arch became a single, isolated arch, a poignant symbol of geological evolution in real-time. Following this unforgettable event, the formation was officially renamed London Arch, reflecting its new, solitary grandeur. This incident serves as a powerful testament to the dynamic nature of our planet.

Fascinating Facts About London Arch

Beyond its dramatic history, London Arch holds several intriguing facts:

  • Geological Marvel: Like many formations along the Great Ocean Road, London Arch is composed of soft limestone, primarily formed from ancient marine organisms. This relatively soft rock is highly susceptible to erosion, explaining its continuous reshaping by the elements.
  • Continuous Evolution: The process that created London Arch and led to its collapse is still ongoing. The arch continues to be eroded by wind, rain, and waves, meaning its appearance will subtly change over geological timescales.
  • Wildlife Haven: The waters around London Arch are teeming with marine life, and the cliffs provide nesting sites for various seabirds, including little penguins and shearwaters.
  • Part of a Larger Tapestry: London Arch is a key feature within the Port Campbell National Park, a protected area renowned for its dramatic coastal scenery and significant geological formations, including the famous Twelve Apostles and Loch Ard Gorge.

Mastering Your Visit: Best Viewing Tips

To truly capture the essence and grandeur of London Arch, consider these expert viewing tips:

  • Golden Hour Magic: The absolute best times for viewing and photography are sunrise and sunset. The low-angle light casts a warm, golden glow on the limestone, enhancing its textures and creating breathtaking photographic opportunities.
  • Multiple Perspectives: London Arch offers two main viewing platforms. The upper platform provides a panoramic vista, while a lower platform offers a closer, more intimate perspective. Take the time to visit both.
  • Photography Prowess: Bring a wide-angle lens to capture the full scope. Experiment with different angles and compositions, including waves crashing against the base.
  • Safety First: Always stay on marked paths and behind safety barriers. The cliffs are unstable. Respect the power of the ocean and never venture onto rocks near the water's edge.
  • Dress for Success: The weather can be unpredictable. Bring layers, including a windproof jacket, and wear comfortable walking shoes.
